# Scaling plan

Generated by `python scripts/40_prep.py scaling-plan` from `src/wxrisk/prep/scaling_plan.py` and the evidence of `scripts/42_scaling_audit.py` and `scripts/43_scaling_kmeans.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Evaluation rules (AGENTS.md): split by year (fit 2010-2016 / 2000-2016, test later), skill against a climatological or prevalence baseline, event- or year-clustered bootstrap intervals; a step is kept only if its gain exceeds the interval. Every parameter below is fitted on the training rows only.

## 1. Which learners need scaling

**The learner decides.** Trees are invariant to monotone transforms and to scale: boosting on transformed features moves within-event AUC by -0.0014 [-0.0042, +0.0011] (reference: raw). They get no scaling; any gain must come from new information, not rescaling.

Logistic regression with an L2 penalty, K-means/GMM and Poisson fits by BFGS are scale-dependent and always get standardised inputs, because:

- *K-means:* with no scaling the column standard deviations differ by a factor of 203407 and the skill collapses (tier B, y0: BSS +0.0009 against +0.0253; difference -0.0244 [-0.0296, -0.0200]; tier C difference -0.0184 [-0.0233, -0.0138]). One column dominates the distance.
- *Logistic regression:* the AUC of the unscaled fit is about the same (0.7817 against 0.7806), which hides the problem: it did not converge within 400 iterations at any C (400 iterations used), against 20-23 when standardised. Convergence is therefore a check in the plan: an unconverged fit is a scaling failure even when the score looks fine.
- With standardised and transformed inputs the C sweep is flat from C = 0.1 up (0.7854 at 0.1, 1 and 10) and only slightly lower below it (0.7836 at 0.001): the data, not the penalty, set the weights, so C = 0.1 is kept.

## 2. Which columns are transformed, and how

**Transform by role, from the training distribution, never by looking at the test years.** Rules: non-negative amounts, depths, rain and counts with skew > 1.5 get `log1p`; signed heavy tails get `asinh`; coordinates, bounded shares and columns that are already logs are only standardised; the month is encoded as sin/cos. The role beats a pure statistic: the skew rule alone would `asinh` the longitude (skew -1.8) and give a meaningless transform of an angle.

Measured effect on the logistic regression (within-event AUC, test 2017-2023, difference to raw+standardised): transforms +0.0032 [-0.0024, +0.0078], plus cyclic month +0.0048 [-0.0009, +0.0096]. Neither interval excludes 0: the transforms are kept for conditioning, weight balance and the cyclic month (a modelling error, not an accuracy trick), not because they won on this test period.

## 3. Group-aware statistics

**Group-aware statistics only where the metric is within-group and the evidence shows a gain.** Two kinds exist (`FeaturePlan`): `fitted` (per-group statistics from the training rows, shrunk toward the global value for small groups, global fallback for an unseen group) and `sample` (statistics of the group inside the batch being transformed, e.g. within-episode z-score or percentile; no label, no other group, but the whole group must be present at prediction time).

Where the variance sits (share between episodes): rain 0.83-0.95 for block-group and neighbourhood rain, 0.60 for log policy count, 0.01 for mean coverage; between years at most 0.09 for any feature. So a *year* normalisation has nothing to remove and is forbidden anyway (its statistics would use the test years). The within-event metric looks only at the 5%-17% of rain variance that is within an episode.

Measured: within-episode z-score and percentile of the rain, exposure and county-relative terrain features added to the logistic regression: +0.0040 [-0.0001, +0.0087] (with transforms and cyclic month alone: +0.0048 [-0.0009, +0.0096]); added to boosting: +0.0028 [-0.0013, +0.0064]. No gain beyond the interval, so the pilot plan carries **no** group-aware column; the existing `f1_vs_cty` (cell rain minus county mean) is the one such feature and stays.

SCS K-means: removing the county x month mean against a plain global z-score: tier B +0.0013 [-0.0018, +0.0045], tier C -0.0016 [-0.0055, +0.0022] (difference of the alternative minus the current anomalies, y0). The county x month anomaly is not demonstrably better than the global z-score; the evidence does not require it. It is kept to leave the published stage 2-5 results unchanged, with its group means fitted on the training years only; a group never seen in training must fall back to the global mean.

## 4. Discretisation

**Discretise only to give a linear or distance-based learner the thresholds a tree has, and only as an extra column.** Cut points come from a shallow tree on the training rows (target-based, at most 8 bins, at least 1% of the rows per bin). Never for trees; never instead of the continuous column: one feature's within-event AUC is invariant to monotone transforms but binning ties the rows inside a bin (bg_f1: 0.7398 continuous against 0.7082 binned).

Measured, added to the transformed + cyclic logistic regression (0.7854): all 12 tested columns +0.0084 [+0.0021, +0.0142] (that run also carries the group-aware columns), by role: exposure columns +0.0041 [+0.0009, +0.0072], rain -0.0003 [-0.0034, +0.0025], geography -0.0014 [-0.0045, +0.0021]; single columns: sfha_share +0.0017 [+0.0005, +0.0030], log_pol +0.0021 [-0.0001, +0.0040], mean_cov +0.0018 [-0.0008, +0.0040].

The gain sits in the exposure columns, where the response has thresholds (share of policies in the special flood hazard area: none against some). Rain and geography gain nothing. **Status: candidate.** 15 comparisons were made on one test period and are not corrected for multiplicity; before the bins enter a model, confirm them with the blocked-by-year cross-validation. The discretisation of the target-free analysis bins (depth classes in script 33, claim-rate classes in script 35) is descriptive and is not part of any model input.

## 5. Targets and the inverse transform

**Targets that were transformed must come back before they are scored or reported; features never come back.** `TargetTransform` keeps the transform, its inverse and the retransformation bias; `Scaled` tags a model output with its space; `scaling.mse / brier / mape_weighted` raise on anything not in original units. A log-scale prediction read back with `exp` is a *geometric mean*, and the mean is larger by the smearing factor; that factor differs by group when the residual spread does (damage ratio by foundation: basement 2.051, crawl 2.211, elevated 2.855, slab 1.958), so it must be estimated per group.

Measured on the damage ratio with the same controls (5 depth bins, loss year), foundation vs slab: ratio of geometric means (log-OLS, what script 30 reports) basement 0.478, crawlspace 0.467, elevated 0.208; ratio of means (log-link GLM) basement 0.502, crawlspace 0.569, elevated 0.327. Stage 9's 'elevated lose 0.27x slab' (state-month fixed effects, other sample) is the first kind too; in this comparison the reduction in dollars is smaller (about 0.327x).
